## Why route existence does not guarantee path availability

A route is a forwarding instruction, not a health check. It tells the router
where to send packets for a prefix, but the router does not verify that the
next hop is alive, that the rest of the path works, or that the destination
can reply. Static routes have no keepalive, so the router only notices
failures of its own interfaces.

In this exercise, disabling ethernet-1/3 removes the connected route
10.1.3.0/24, so the next hop 10.1.3.2 can no longer be resolved. The static
route stays in the configuration, but traffic to 10.1.5.0/24 is dropped.
If instead the link stayed up and the next-hop device failed, the route
would remain active and packets would be silently dropped (a black hole).
Detecting that needs routing protocols or BFD, and end-to-end tests such as
ping and traceroute are needed to confirm a path really works.
